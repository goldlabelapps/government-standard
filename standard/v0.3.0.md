# Government Standard for AI-Assisted Software Engineering

**Version:** 0.3.0-draft  
**Status:** Public draft for review  
**Published:** 24 November 2026  
**Owner:** Government Standard project

This is an independent proposal. It is not government-endorsed, a certification scheme, or a substitute for applicable law, contracts, security policies, classification rules or formal assurance.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This standard defines a baseline for controlling AI systems used to perform or assist software engineering work. It covers chat-based assistants, code completion systems and agents that can read or change files, execute commands, or use APIs and other tools.

It applies to the organisation authorising the use and to people acting on its behalf. It concerns AI used in the engineering process, not AI capabilities in the resulting software. It does not approve any provider, model, data classification or use case.

Before using an AI system, the organisation remains responsible for determining whether the use is lawful, contractually permitted and consistent with its own security and information-handling requirements. If those requirements are stricter than this draft, the stricter requirements apply. Nothing here authorises processing information in a system or environment that is not approved for that information.

## 2. Normative language and application

**MUST** and **MUST NOT** state requirements for conformance with this draft. **SHOULD** and **SHOULD NOT** state recommendations that may be departed from where the organisation documents its rationale and risk decision. **MAY** indicates an option.

Unless a control is explicitly conditional, it applies to every in-scope AI use. An organisation may determine that a control does not apply to a specific use only after recording the rationale and approval. A departure from a MUST is an exception to this draft, not conformance with that requirement. Exceptions cannot override law, contract, classification policy or other binding organisational requirements.

This draft defines no maturity score or certification. A statement of conformance should identify the exact version, scope, applicable controls, exceptions and evidence reviewed.

## 3. Controls

### GOV — Governance and approval

#### GS-AI-GOV-01 — Authorise each use

**Requirement — MUST:** Before use, an accountable organisational authority MUST approve the use case, named user or team, AI service and model (where identifiable), intended task, permitted information classes, enabled tools, and responsible owner.

**Implementation:** Maintain an inventory or approval record. A provider's general availability or an individual's account is not organisational approval.

**Evidence:** Dated approval and an inventory entry with scope and owner.

#### GS-AI-GOV-02 — Assess provider and service

**Requirement — MUST:** Before approval, the organisation MUST assess whether the provider and service meet its requirements for data handling, retention and deletion, model-training use, access control, incident notification, service location where relevant, and contractual terms.

**Implementation:** Verify current service settings and terms; do not infer protections from marketing statements. Reassess when the provider, service, terms or material processing behaviour changes.

**Evidence:** Assessment record, applicable terms, and relevant service configuration.

### DATA — Information handling

#### GS-AI-DATA-01 — Classify and minimise inputs

**Requirement — MUST:** Users MUST determine the classification and sensitivity of information before making it available to an AI service, including through prompts, attachments, repository context, connected tools and agent-accessible files. They MUST minimise information to what is necessary and use it only where the service and processing path are approved for that information.

**Implementation:** Apply the organisation's classification policy. Redact or synthesise data where feasible. Treat a provider's training opt-out or a private workspace as a setting, not as authorisation to process otherwise prohibited information.

**Evidence:** Approved data-handling rules and, where required, a recorded classification decision.

#### GS-AI-DATA-02 — Protect restricted data and secrets

**Requirement — MUST:** Users and agents MUST NOT expose credentials, secrets, personal data or protected information to an AI service unless the specific service, data, purpose and processing path have been explicitly authorised under applicable policy.

**Implementation:** Exclude secret stores and sensitive paths from agent context; use synthetic or redacted examples. If exposure is suspected, follow the incident process in GS-AI-INC-01.

**Evidence:** Access configuration, approved handling procedure and relevant review records.

### TOOL — Service and tool configuration

#### GS-AI-TOOL-01 — Record the deployed configuration

**Requirement — MUST:** The organisation MUST record the service, model or version where available, extensions, plugins, tool integrations, and security-relevant settings for each approved use. It MUST review material changes before continued use.

**Implementation:** Pin versions where supported. If the provider changes models without exposing versions, document that limitation and define a review or disablement trigger.

**Evidence:** Configuration snapshot or inventory entry and change-review records.

#### GS-AI-TOOL-02 — Review extensions and integrations

**Requirement — MUST:** Each extension, plugin, MCP server, API integration or other tool connected to an AI system MUST be approved before it is enabled, with its permissions and data flows assessed.

**Implementation:** Disable unused tools. Assess the tool's source, maintenance, permissions, network destinations and handling of data returned to or sent from the model.

**Evidence:** Tool approval, permission list and data-flow assessment.

### ACCESS — Identity and least privilege

#### GS-AI-ACCESS-01 — Limit access to task scope

**Requirement — MUST:** AI systems and agents MUST receive only the filesystem, repository, account and service access necessary for the approved task. Access MUST be limited to an explicitly authorised workspace or resource set.

**Implementation:** Prefer isolated workspaces or sandboxes. Deny access by default to unrelated repositories, home directories, credential files, production systems and shared administrative locations. Verify that configured exclusions are enforced by the execution environment, not merely described in a prompt.

**Evidence:** Policy or sandbox configuration and a record of granted access.

#### GS-AI-ACCESS-02 — Constrain network access

**Requirement — MUST:** Network access available to an AI agent MUST be disabled unless required for the approved task. Required access MUST be restricted to approved destinations and protocols where technically feasible.

**Implementation:** Use network-layer controls such as an egress proxy or allowlist; a prompt asking an agent not to connect is not an access control. Document unavoidable broader access and its risk treatment.

**Evidence:** Network policy, allowlist or documented exception with approval.

#### GS-AI-ACCESS-03 — Do not grant ambient credentials

**Requirement — MUST:** Agents MUST NOT receive unrestricted, persistent or ambient credentials. Credentials required for a task MUST be scoped to the minimum resource and action, delivered through an approved mechanism, and revoked or expired when no longer needed.

**Implementation:** Prefer short-lived credentials and delegated access. Do not place credentials in prompts, source files, command history or agent-readable configuration.

**Evidence:** Identity configuration, credential lifetime and scope, and revocation records where applicable.

### EXEC — Execution boundaries

#### GS-AI-EXEC-01 — Isolate execution

**Requirement — MUST:** An agent that can execute commands or modify files MUST operate within an execution boundary that restricts its filesystem, process and network access to the approved task scope.

**Implementation:** Use an organisation-approved sandbox, container, virtual machine or equivalent enforcement mechanism. If the environment cannot enforce the boundary, disable the relevant capability or do not use the agent for that task. A system prompt alone is not enforcement.

**Evidence:** Environment configuration and documented boundary validation.

#### GS-AI-EXEC-02 — Treat content and tool output as untrusted

**Requirement — MUST:** Users and system designers MUST treat prompts, repository content, issues, documentation, tool results and external responses as untrusted input, including content that instructs an agent to disclose data, change its rules or invoke tools.

**Implementation:** Do not rely on model behaviour to neutralise prompt injection. Restrict tool permissions and validate consequential actions independently of instructions found in retrieved content.

**Evidence:** Tool policy and documented handling or testing of untrusted content.

### HUMAN — Human oversight and approvals

#### GS-AI-HUM-01 — Require human authorisation for consequential actions

**Requirement — MUST:** A responsible human MUST review and authorise actions that publish or transmit information externally, deploy or change production systems, alter security or access controls, create financial or legal commitments, or irreversibly destroy data. An agent MUST NOT approve its own action.

**Implementation:** Enforce approval at the tool, platform or workflow boundary where available. Do not treat an agent's statement that approval was obtained as approval evidence.

**Evidence:** Approval record linked to the action and identity of the approver.

#### GS-AI-HUM-02 — Retain accountable human ownership

**Requirement — MUST:** Each AI-assisted work item MUST have a named human who is responsible for scope, review and acceptance of the resulting work. Delegation to an AI system does not transfer professional or organisational accountability.

**Implementation:** Identify the responsible person in the normal issue, change or work-tracking process. The owner should have sufficient competence and authority to assess the output.

**Evidence:** Work item or change record identifying its human owner.

### CODE — Software integrity and verification

#### GS-AI-CODE-01 — Review AI-generated changes

**Requirement — MUST:** AI-generated or AI-modified code and configuration MUST be reviewed by a human before acceptance, using the same or stronger review controls as comparable human-authored changes.

**Implementation:** Review the actual diff, including generated files, tests, infrastructure and policy changes. Give particular scrutiny to authentication, authorisation, cryptography, data handling and security boundaries.

**Evidence:** Review approval associated with the change.

#### GS-AI-CODE-02 — Verify changes with project controls

**Requirement — MUST:** Before acceptance, changes MUST be checked using the project's applicable tests and quality or security checks. Results MUST be inspected; failed, skipped or unavailable checks MUST be disclosed and resolved or explicitly accepted by an authorised human under normal project risk procedures.

**Implementation:** Record exact commands and outcomes. Add or update tests for behaviour changed by the work. An agent's assertion that checks passed is not evidence.

**Evidence:** Test and analysis results linked to the change, plus documented disposition of failures.

#### GS-AI-CODE-03 — Preserve review and build protections

**Requirement — MUST:** AI tools MUST NOT bypass required code review, branch protection, signed change, build, release or deployment controls.

**Implementation:** Keep enforcement outside the agent where possible. Do not grant agents permission to weaken controls governing their own output.

**Evidence:** Repository and pipeline settings, and relevant audit records.

### SUPPLY — Dependencies and external components

#### GS-AI-SUPPLY-01 — Assess suggested dependencies and tools

**Requirement — MUST:** Dependencies, packages, models, plugins and executable tools proposed or installed through AI-assisted work MUST undergo the same organisation-approved source, maintenance, licensing and security checks as other additions.

**Implementation:** Use approved registries and normal lockfile, provenance and vulnerability-management controls. Do not install or execute an unreviewed component solely because an AI recommended it.

**Evidence:** Dependency review, lockfile and relevant scan or approval results.

### LOG — Records and audit

#### GS-AI-LOG-01 — Keep proportionate activity records

**Requirement — MUST:** The organisation MUST retain records sufficient to determine who used which approved AI service for what work, the relevant configuration and permissions, consequential approvals or exceptions, and the verification and review outcome.

**Implementation:** Link records to the work item or change. Apply existing retention and access rules. Do not record full prompts or responses by default if they may contain sensitive data; protect or redact records that do contain it.

**Evidence:** Audit or work records and the applicable retention and access policy.

### INCIDENT — Incident response

#### GS-AI-INC-01 — Respond to AI-related incidents

**Requirement — MUST:** Suspected disclosure, unauthorised access or action, unsafe tool behaviour, or compromise involving an AI service MUST be handled under the organisation's incident process. That process MUST support prompt containment, access revocation where applicable, notification to the responsible security authority, and preservation of relevant evidence.

**Implementation:** Include AI services and connected tools in incident reporting and response procedures. Determine whether provider notification or contractual reporting is required.

**Evidence:** Incident procedure and, where an event occurred, incident records and corrective actions.

### REVIEW — Ongoing assurance

#### GS-AI-REVIEW-01 — Reassess continued use

**Requirement — MUST:** The owner MUST reassess an approved AI use at least annually and after a material change to the model, provider, service terms, connected tools, data processed, permissions, threat context or applicable policy. Use MUST be suspended or narrowed when required controls are no longer met.

**Implementation:** Define change triggers and a review owner. Where a provider's changes cannot be observed, account for that uncertainty in approval and monitoring.

**Evidence:** Dated reassessment, changes made and approval to continue.

#### GS-AI-REVIEW-02 — Review and version the standard

**Requirement — SHOULD:** The project SHOULD review this standard at least annually and following material changes in technology, threats or relevant authoritative guidance. Each published revision SHOULD identify its status, date, changes and rationale.

**Implementation:** Use the contribution and review process in this repository. Do not imply that a revision has been endorsed by an external authority.

**Evidence:** Public version history and review record.

## 4. Evidence and exceptions

Evidence should be sufficient for an independent reviewer to determine whether a control is implemented and operating. It may include approvals, service and sandbox settings, access lists, workflow records, test results and incident procedures. Evidence collection must follow the same data classification and retention rules as other organisational records.

An exception to a MUST in this draft should identify the affected use and control, the reason, risk assessment, compensating controls, accountable approver and expiry or review date. The exception must be approved before the affected use. It does not establish conformance with the unmet control and cannot authorise a use prohibited by an external obligation.

## 5. References and relationship to other frameworks

The following sources inform this draft. They are references, not endorsements, and their inclusion does not mean this standard is equivalent to or compliant with them. Their requirements apply only where independently applicable. Sources were checked on 23 November 2026; consult the current editions and guidance before use.

- [NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) — voluntary risk-management framework; NIST notes the framework is being revised.
- [NIST AI RMF Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1)](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1) — generative AI risk-management resource.
- [NIST Secure Software Development Framework (SSDF), SP 800-218](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/218/final) — secure software development practices.
- [UK Government Security Classifications](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/government-security-classifications) — applies within its stated UK government context; follow the current policy and organisational implementation.
- [OWASP Agentic AI — Threats and Mitigations](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/agentic-ai-threats-and-mitigations/) — threat-model-based guidance for agentic systems.
- [ISO/IEC 27001](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001) and [ISO/IEC 42001](https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html) — information-security and AI-management-system standards. Consult the current editions and applicable licensing.
